# Refund reply

Use this when answering a customer about a return, a refund, or where their
order is. The governed step supplies the facts; this decides the shape of the
reply.

## Shape

Four short paragraphs, in this order. No headings, no bullet lists.

1. **The decision, first line.** "Yes, this can be returned." / "This is outside
   the return window." / "The parcel is still in transit." Never open with an
   apology or a restatement of the question.
2. **The order, named.** Order id, what it was, and the date that decided the
   outcome — the delivery date for a return, the shipment scan for a delivery
   question.
3. **The rule, in plain words.** Quote the verified policy rule you applied and
   its retrieved source. One sentence. Do not paste the handbook.
4. **The single next step**, and who takes it. If the next step is someone
   else's — a manager, the logistics desk — say who and how long they take.

## Rules

- One decision per reply. If two things are true (returnable, but above the
  approval threshold), lead with the eligibility and make the approval the next
  step.
- Never promise money you cannot issue. When the verified policy requires
  approval, say "eligible, pending approval", not "your refund is on the way".
- Claim a refund, label, or escalation was completed only when a tool result
  confirms it. The tutorial's tracker is read-only: describe the next action
  and its owner instead of claiming it acted.
- Use order facts only from actual lookup results and policy rules only from
  retrieved policy. This style guide supplies neither. If a lookup is
  unavailable, say so; do not decide eligibility or claim the order lacks data.
- If the policy and the order data disagree, say so plainly and escalate rather
  than picking one.
- British or American spelling, matched to the customer's own.
